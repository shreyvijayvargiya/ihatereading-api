# Pricing Intelligence

## Mission

Turn a SaaS website and its competitors into a **pricing intelligence report**.

Use when the user asks:

> "Analyze our pricing."

> "Compare our pricing with competitors."

> "What should we charge?"

> "Find pricing opportunities in this market."

Do not simply collect prices. Understand **packaging, value, limits, positioning, and competitive differences**.

---

## 1. INPUT

### Required

* User's SaaS URL

### Optional

* Competitor URLs/names
* Target market
* Target customer
* Current pricing
* Desired pricing model
* Specific pricing question

Research missing information instead of asking unnecessary questions.

---

## 2. UNDERSTAND THE PRODUCT

Research:

* Product
* Core features
* Target customers
* Use cases
* Pricing model
* Free plan/trial
* Existing plans
* Usage metrics
* Feature limits
* Add-ons
* Enterprise offering
* Billing frequency

Determine what the customer is actually paying for:

* seats
* usage
* features
* transactions
* storage
* credits
* outcomes
* other value metrics

---

## 3. COMPETITOR PRICING RESEARCH

Research relevant competitors.

For each competitor capture:

* Starting price
* Plan names
* Billing model
* Free tier
* Trial
* Main limits
* Feature gates
* Usage limits
* Enterprise pricing
* Add-ons
* Discounts where publicly available

Prefer official pricing pages.

Clearly mark pricing that requires contacting sales.

Do not invent unavailable prices.

---

## 4. PACKAGING ANALYSIS

Compare what each company puts into each tier.

Identify:

* Common features
* Premium features
* Artificial limits
* Usage limits
* Seat limits
* Upgrade triggers
* Enterprise-only capabilities

Determine:

> What causes customers to move from one plan to another?

---

## 5. VALUE METRIC ANALYSIS

Identify the strongest pricing metric for the category.

Examples:

* seats
* projects
* contacts
* API usage
* credits
* transactions
* revenue
* storage

Determine whether the user's current metric aligns with customer value.

Do not recommend changing pricing metrics without evidence.

---

## 6. MARKET POSITIONING

Classify the user's pricing relative to competitors:

* Budget
* Affordable
* Mid-market
* Premium
* Enterprise

Then determine whether pricing supports the product's positioning.

Look for opportunities such as:

* missing entry tier
* weak free tier
* poor upgrade path
* excessive feature gating
* unclear value
* confusing plans
* pricing significantly above/below comparable products

---

## 7. OUTPUT

Return:

### Pricing Summary

* Current pricing
* Pricing model
* Market position
* Major strengths
* Major weaknesses

### Competitor Comparison

| Company | Starting Price | Free | Main Metric | Premium Trigger | Position |
| ------- | -------------: | ---- | ----------- | --------------- | -------- |

### Packaging Analysis

Explain what competitors include in each tier.

### Opportunities

List:

* Pricing opportunities
* Packaging opportunities
* Upgrade opportunities
* Value-metric opportunities
* Positioning opportunities

### Recommendations

Give prioritized recommendations:

**Do now**

**Test**

**Consider later**

Never present a pricing recommendation as guaranteed optimal pricing.

---

## QUALITY RULES

* Prefer current official pricing.
* Include the date/context of pricing research when useful.
* Do not fabricate prices.
* Do not confuse monthly and annual pricing.
* Separate public pricing from sales-only pricing.
* Do not recommend pricing solely because a competitor charges more or less.
* Consider customer value, positioning, and product maturity.
